> Module 02 / 06 · ~3–5 WEEKS

Ethics, Bias & Safety in AI and LLMs

What can go wrong, who decides, and how to think clearly about AI.

Approximately three to five weeks, depending on the instructor's campus pacing calendar. Part of Deep Learning or Delulu: Why Large Language Models Make Stuff Up — a six-module curriculum for secondary students in grades 6–12.

WEEKS: 01 02 03 04 05
Week 01

Frameworks and Vocabulary

Students build the shared language and frameworks they will use for the rest of the module, learning to separate three often-confused ideas — ethics, bias, and safety — and meet the field's foundational thinkers.

What students learn

  1. Three definitions. Ethics asks what a system should do. Bias asks what systematic errors it makes. Safety asks what harms it can cause.
  2. The Five Big Ideas in AI revisited, with focus on Big Idea Five — Societal Impact.
  3. Stakeholders — developers, users, and the third parties affected by the system who never chose to interact with it.
  4. Joy Buolamwini and the Gender Shades project, 2018.
  5. Timnit Gebru and Emily Bender — revisited from Module 1, with the Stochastic Parrots argument unpacked.
  6. Class norm-setting for hard topics — disagree with ideas, not people.

Key resources

  • Joy Buolamwini, Unmasking AI (2023) — Chapters 1 and 2.
  • Joy Buolamwini TED Talk — How I'm fighting bias in algorithms.
  • Coded Bias (2020 documentary) — selected segments.
  • AI4K12.org, Big Idea Five progression charts for grades 6 to 8 and 9 to 12.
  • MIT RAISE, Day of AI ethics lesson.
  • Common Sense Education, AI Literacy ethics lessons.
Week 02

Where Bias Comes From

Students learn that bias in AI isn't a glitch — it enters through the data the model learns from, the choices designers make, and the contexts where the system gets deployed.

What students learn

  1. The principle of "garbage in, garbage out."
  2. Data bias — who is in the dataset, who is missing.
  3. Design bias — what the developers chose to optimize for.
  4. Deployment bias — using a model in a context it wasn't built for.
  5. Case study — Amazon's scrapped hiring algorithm, 2018.
  6. Case study — the COMPAS recidivism algorithm and ProPublica's 2016 investigation.
  7. Case study — healthcare algorithm bias, Obermeyer and others, 2019.
  8. Hands-on dataset audit activity.
  9. Begin the Ethics Case File wall.

Key resources

  • Cathy O'Neil, Weapons of Math Destruction (2016).
  • ProPublica — Machine Bias.
  • Obermeyer and others, "Dissecting racial bias in an algorithm" — Science (2019).
  • Reuters — Amazon scraps secret AI recruiting tool.
  • MIT Moral Machine.
Week 03

Safety and What "Harm" Means

Students move from bias to safety, categorizing the things that can go wrong with AI systems from small annoyances to serious damage.

What students learn

  1. Hallucination — when the model states false things confidently.
  2. Misinformation and deepfakes.
  3. Privacy harms — what not to share with a chatbot.
  4. Dependency and skill atrophy.
  5. Manipulation and engagement-driven design.
  6. Case study — Mata v. Avianca, the lawyer who cited hallucinated cases, 2023.
  7. Case study — deepfake incidents in schools.
  8. Class poster — what is safe to share with a chatbot, in green, yellow, and red.
  9. Activity — students classify potential harms in their own recent AI prompts.

Key resources

  • MIT Media Lab — Detect Fakes.
  • Common Sense Education, "Deepfakes, Distrust, and Democracy" lesson.
  • Mozilla — Privacy Not Included.
  • New York Times, "Here's What Happens When Your Lawyer Uses ChatGPT" (May 2023).
  • Anthropic Usage Policy and OpenAI Usage Policies.
Week 04

Governance, Regulation & Responsibility

Students learn that AI ethics isn't only a technical problem — it's a question of who decides, who is accountable, and what rules societies are writing right now.

What students learn

  1. Three layers of responsibility — the developer, the deployer, the user.
  2. The EU AI Act, 2024 — the first comprehensive AI law.
  3. The U.S. Executive Order on AI, 2023, and its rollback in 2025.
  4. State-level action — Colorado, California, and AI in schools.
  5. Stuart Russell and the control problem.
  6. Dario and Daniela Amodei and Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy.
  7. Red-teaming — the discipline of trying to break a model before it ships.
  8. Class debate — should secondary schools allow AI use on homework? Students argue assigned positions.

Key resources

  • Stuart Russell, Human Compatible (2019).
  • Stuart Russell TED Talk — 3 principles for creating safer AI.
  • Anthropic — Responsible Scaling Policy.
  • EU AI Act explorer.
  • AI4K12 Societal Impact lessons for grades 6 to 12.
  • ISTE — AI in Education policy briefs.
Week 05

Building Your Own Ethical-Use Framework

Students consolidate the module into a working personal framework they will carry into the technical modules that follow.

What students learn

  1. Review of the four anchor concepts — ethics, bias, safety, governance.
  2. The AI Use Checklist — students draft a 5 to 7 item checklist in their own words.
  3. Stakeholder mapping exercise on one real-world AI deployment.
  4. Position paper — one to two pages on how the student will use AI differently because of the module.
  5. Gallery walk with structured peer response.
  6. Module 2 assessment — vocabulary, case-study identification, and a written defense of the student's own checklist.
  7. Bridge to Module 3.

Key resources

  • Student work from Weeks 1 to 4 — the Ethics Case File wall and vocabulary list.
  • Anthropic Usage Policy and OpenAI Usage Policies, re-read as models for the checklist.
  • Common Sense Education AI Literacy capstone lesson.
  • MIT RAISE Day of AI capstone activity.
  • Teacher-provided position-paper rubric.
