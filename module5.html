> Module 05 / 06 · ~3–5 WEEKS

Pretrain, Finetune, Modify & Evaluate

The full model lifecycle — from raw internet to a model students can audit and defend.

Approximately three to five weeks, depending on the instructor's campus pacing calendar. Part of Deep Learning or Delulu: Why Large Language Models Make Stuff Up — a six-module curriculum for secondary students in grades 6–12.

WEEKS: 01 02 03 04 05
Week 01

Pretraining

Students learn that the difference between their tiny Shakespeare-trained GPT and ChatGPT isn't the architecture — it's the scale and source of the data the model learned from.

What students learn

  1. Pretraining defined — the first and most expensive stage.
  2. The internet as training data — Common Crawl, Wikipedia, books, GitHub.
  3. The scale problem — compute, time, and energy.
  4. What pretraining produces — a base model, and how it differs from an assistant.
  5. Data curation — the invisible work of quality filtering and deduplication.
  6. Kaplan and colleagues, 2020 — scaling laws.
  7. Hoffmann and colleagues, 2022 — the Chinchilla correction.
  8. Begin the lifecycle diagram on the wall.

Key resources

  • Andrej Karpathy — Intro to Large Language Models — pretraining sections.
  • Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard.
  • Common Crawl.
  • Teacher-prepared base-model demo.
  • Kaplan and others — Scaling Laws (2020).
  • Hoffmann and others — Chinchilla (2022).
  • The Pile and RedPajama.
Week 02

Finetuning

Students learn how a useless-feeling base model is transformed into the helpful, instruction-following assistants they actually use, and the role human feedback plays.

What students learn

  1. The gap between pretraining and usefulness, demonstrated side by side.
  2. Supervised Fine-Tuning — imitating human question-and-answer examples.
  3. Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback — the model learns taste.
  4. InstructGPT, 2022 — Long Ouyang, Paul Christiano, and the paper behind ChatGPT.
  5. Constitutional AI — Anthropic's principle-based approach.
  6. The human labor behind finetuning.
  7. Hands-on — finetune a small model on a custom author corpus.
  8. Update the lifecycle diagram — Finetune stage fills in.

Key resources

  • Ouyang and others — InstructGPT (2022).
  • Hugging Face NLP Course.

  • OpenAI and Anthropic model cards.
Week 03

Modifying Models

Students learn the many ways a finished model can be modified for specific uses without retraining it from scratch, and why these techniques matter for anyone who builds with AI.

What students learn

  1. Three levels of modification — prompting, RAG, and adapters.
  2. Prompt engineering as a real skill — system prompts, few-shot, chain-of-thought.
  3. Build a personal prompt library.
  4. Retrieval-Augmented Generation explained — search plus generation.
  5. Hands-on RAG project over a corpus students choose.
  6. LoRA and adapters — cheap, stackable modifications.
  7. The industry pattern — frontier model plus your data.
  8. Update the lifecycle diagram — Modify stage fills in.

Key resources

  • OpenAI — Prompt engineering guide.
  • Anthropic — Prompt engineering.

  • LangChain or LlamaIndex.

Week 04

Evaluation

Students learn the science — and the current crisis — of evaluating LLMs, including standard benchmarks, their weaknesses, and the practitioner's craft of building your own evals.

What students learn

  1. The central problem — how do you measure something that can do almost anything?
  2. Standard benchmarks — MMLU, HumanEval, GSM8K, HellaSwag, ARC, TruthfulQA.
  3. The leaderboard reality — Hugging Face, Chatbot Arena, Artificial Analysis.
  4. Why benchmarks lie — contamination, saturation, and gaming.
  5. Chatbot Arena and human preference evaluation. Students participate.
  6. Build your own eval — 10 test cases for a task the student cares about, tested on 2 or 3 models.
  7. The transfer to data science evaluation more broadly.
  8. Update the lifecycle diagram — Evaluate stage fills in.

Key resources

  • Chatbot Arena.
  • Hugging Face Open LLM Leaderboard.
  • Artificial Analysis.
  • Hendrycks and others — MMLU (2020).
  • Chen and others — HumanEval (2021).
  • Anthropic evaluation documentation.
  • OpenAI Evals.
  • Teacher-prepared "Build Your Own Eval" template.
Week 05

Model Audit and Synthesis

Students consolidate the full model lifecycle, apply it to a model of their choosing, and produce a real audit deliverable.

What students learn

  1. Walk the lifecycle diagram end-to-end alongside the Module 3 and Module 4 artifacts.
  2. Choose one real current model to audit — Claude, GPT-4, Llama, Mistral, or Gemma.
  3. Research what is publicly known about the model's pretraining.
  4. Research what is publicly known about the model's finetuning.
  5. Identify what user modifications are available.
  6. Score the model on two benchmarks and interpret the results.
  7. Hands-on testing — identify one task it does well and one it does poorly.
  8. Module 5 assessment — vocabulary, lifecycle trace, and application to real problems.
  9. Peer audit exchange with one written question each.
  10. Bridge to Module 6.

Question 1: Based only on these scores, which model should a student pick to help rewrite a paragraph at a 4th-grade reading level?


Show the discussion

Trick question. This benchmark cannot answer this. A general knowledge test measures academic breadth across dozens of subjects — not reading-level rewriting, not tone, not clarity for young readers. A model could score 88 here and still be worse at 4th-grade rewriting than a model that scores 70. This is why Week 4 introduces the idea of building your own eval for the specific task you actually care about.

Question 2: Model A and Human Expert are close — 88 versus 90. Does this mean the model reasons like an expert?


Show the discussion

Not necessarily. Two honest problems from Week 4 apply here. Contamination — the model may have seen very similar questions during training, which inflates the score without proving real understanding. Saturation — once most frontier models cluster near the top, the test stops distinguishing meaningfully between them. In fact, by the time you're reading this, the field may have already moved past this exact test — MMLU itself is increasingly described as “saturated,” with harder tests like GPQA Diamond and SWE-Bench Verified taking its place as the more discriminating measure. A close score to a human expert on any one benchmark is evidence of something, but it is never proof of expert-level reasoning in the real world.

Question 3: How would you find out which real models are leading right now, and on which tests?

Show the answer

Check a live, actively-maintained leaderboard rather than trusting a number in a textbook, a slide deck, or a website like this one. Two good starting points: the Artificial Analysis LLM Leaderboard, which tracks intelligence, price, and speed across 250+ models, and LMArena (formerly Chatbot Arena), which ranks models by blind human preference rather than multiple-choice tests alone. Notice which benchmark each site leads with — that choice itself tells you something about what that source thinks matters most right now.
